Cântara – water vessel

Museu Etnográfico da Casa da Cultura de Vimioso (Portugal) · MECC.0193 · 3D documentation by Patrícia Cordeiro · October 2026

View on Sketchfab

About the object

This cântara, from the ethnographic collection of the Casa da Cultura de Vimioso, is made of unglazed clay. On the 3D model, scaled with a ruler, it is 27.3 cm high and about 21 cm wide, handle included (inventory: 27 × 24 × 20.5 cm; the width is being checked). It has a rounded body, a narrow neck, a single handle from the rim to the shoulder, and a band of short incised strokes around the shoulder. [Other marks or signs of use – to confirm.]

Its decoration, surface texture and shape suggest that it may have been made by the women potters (louceiras) of Pinela (Bragança; see the Centro Interpretativo da Cerâmica de Pinela). This is a hypothesis based on comparison with Pinela pottery, not a confirmed attribution; it is being reviewed in the museum's inventory work.

Intangible heritage

Pottery know-how. As a clay vessel, the cântara is first of all the product of the know-how of Portuguese pottery. In the terms of the UNESCO 2003 Convention, it belongs to the domain of traditional craftsmanship. If the Pinela attribution is confirmed, the object also documents a women's pottery tradition of the Bragança region.

Uses. The cântara was used to carry and keep drinking water, at home and in the fields, where it is part of the memory of the harvest.

Associated practices. The museum's intangible cultural heritage (ICH) inventory singles out cântaros as special objects: going to the fountain was an everyday practice associated with women, linked to fountain songs and to social life in the village.

Community voice

In an interview recorded for the museum on 8 June 2026, basket maker Ema Vila-Chã (79, Vale de Pena) remembered her own clay cântaro, more than 80 years old. She also explained that woven rings (rodelas) were made to rest the cântaro and the large cooking pots on.

“The cântaro that rolled down the slope” – Ema Vila-Chã

Select annotation 3 on the 3D model, or press play. In Portuguese, 52 s.

English translation

“I have one that is more than 80 years old, a clay cântaro. Let me tell you its story. My sister and I were harvesting. We were in the shade, and the cântaro was there too, in the shade of a cork oak. My sister asked for water, and my parents told her: ‘The cântaro is there, drink.’ She touched it and it began to roll down the slope, from stone to stone, until it reached the path. The women harvesting on the other side shouted: ‘Go there, Madalena, there it is, it has reached the path.’ And the blessed cântaro did not break. It doesn't have a single crack. And it is made of clay. I still have it.”

Excerpt from the interview of 8 June 2026 (MUVIP project; interviewers Matilde Gomes, Patrícia Cordeiro and João Marques), 13:23–14:15, volume normalised. Published with the interviewee's signed consent.

Her story is about a cântaro of her own, not this object; it shows the everyday uses and memories that objects like this one carry.
